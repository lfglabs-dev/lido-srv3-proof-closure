# P-ADDRESS-1

> Round 2 (2026-08-21). Product note plus proof audit, arbitrated from GPT 5.6 Pro and Opus 5. Fable 5 was unavailable (data-retention gate). Kimi K3 was not an allowed Task model. No em dashes. Lean is authority.

Four Lido entrypoints let an ordinary account move an address-keyed position: `WithdrawalQueueERC721.transferFrom`, `WithdrawalQueue.requestWithdrawals`, `WithdrawalQueue.claimWithdrawalsTo`, and `WstETH.unwrap`. P-ADDRESS-1 asks whether admission or the committed post-state depends on *which* account the caller is, as opposed to that account's own data.

For nonzero $a_1, a_2$, let $\rho$ swap them and fix every other address. `universal_address_writer_equivariance` is:

- admission: $\mathrm{succeeds}(\mathrm{run}(\rho \cdot \mathrm{inp})) = \mathrm{succeeds}(\mathrm{run}(\mathrm{inp}))$
- post-state: $\mathrm{run}(\mathrm{inp}) = \mathrm{committed}(p) \Rightarrow \mathrm{run}(\rho \cdot \mathrm{inp}) = \mathrm{committed}(\rho \cdot p)$

Both hold for every input and every one of the four tags, with no scope premise. `claimWithdrawalsTo` is in because `caller = requestOwner` is caller-relative: `renameInput` renames both. This parent remains a single-item reading. The separate `AddressClaimBatchTx` incrementally models the live parallel-array loop and storage/CALL surface without widening this theorem.

The kill-line adds `caller == fixedOwner`, a constant $\rho$ does not move: caller 1 commits, caller 2 is rejected. `singletonActorEntryPoint` is `False` for every modeled tag, so singleton-actor writers are excluded by omission. The child `P-ADDRESS-1.denote-admission` is an audit-authored `claim()` under `denoteFunction`, not this four-writer result.

## Proof limitations and recommendations

The parent is a genuine unbounded $\forall$ over a handwritten 18-field record. Twelve environment Booleans are swap-invariant by construction. Equivariance therefore certifies that the projection introduced no unrenamed address constant. Faithfulness to `core@af095e48` is `A-SOURCE-SHAPED`. The separate executable claim transaction accepts arbitrary request/hint lists, reads current and previous cumulative request words and checkpoint words, decodes the packed owner/claimed word, updates the claimed byte and locked ETH, and produces ordered value-bearing empty-calldata payout CALL journal entries. `bounded_live_claim_batch_storage_call_surface` checks a two-item receipt and universal failed-run snapshot rollback. `fixed_payout_recipient_mutant_kill_line` rejects a fixed payout destination.

That increment is not keccak/machine-storage refinement: Verity keyed mapping channels represent the two consecutive queue/checkpoint struct words at `POSITION` and `POSITION + 1`. The checked observe correspondence is bounded to two requests. EnumerableSet removal, events, adversarial recipient code, unbounded batch source/equivariance correspondence, wrapper-only `transferFrom`, Boolean environments on the original four projections, and deployed correspondence remain open. Permissionless admission is proved for only two of the four writers.

CHECKED does not mean pinned Solidity, the full claim batch, singleton-actor exclusion by proof, or Yul/EVM.

Ranked next work: generalize the live-batch source/equivariance correspondence, then bridge physical keccak mapping layout and the remaining owner-set/event/callee semantics without adding singleton-actor functions; do not call all four writers permissionless.

Theorems: `PAddress1.universal_address_writer_equivariance` (parent), `PAddress1.abstract_source_verity_tx_address_equivariance` (four projections), `PAddress1.bounded_live_claim_batch_storage_call_surface` (live-shaped batch increment), `AddressSourceMutants.fixed_owner_gate_kill_line_refutes_parent` and `AddressSourceMutants.fixed_payout_recipient_mutant_kill_line` (kill-lines).

## Intent

Address-bearing Lido entrypoint projections should treat two ordinary nonzero callers symmetrically: swapping callers and their address-indexed inputs should preserve admission and rename successful post-states. Since wave 5 the parent covers four modeled writers, including `WithdrawalQueue.claimWithdrawalsTo` (`addressEquivarianceEntryScope` is total). Permissionless admission is only claimed for `requestWithdrawals` / `unwrap`.

## Modeling

- **SOURCE** `SolidityAddress.run` over four pinned single-item writers; since wave 5 the parent covers all four (`addressEquivarianceEntryScope` is total, `addressEquivarianceEntryScope_total`). `claimWithdrawalsTo` is in scope because its request-owner gate (`caller = requestOwner`) is caller-relative and renames with the caller (`requestOwner` is an address-indexed input field renamed by `renameInput`), so `run_rename` and its corollaries apply there; the wave-4 `hScope` case-split exclusion is retired with the premise.
- **Permissionless admission** on pause/balance paths: `pause_balance_admitted_is_permissionless` for `requestWithdrawals` and `unwrap`.
- **VERITY** four `Contract.run` programs with `observeAddress = sourceAddressView` under the same amount/bounds side conditions and snapshot rollback.
- **Live claim batch VERITY increment** `AddressClaimBatchTx.executeClaimWithdrawalsTo` checks the zero recipient and parallel-list lengths, recursively executes every request/hint pair, reads the pinned queue/checkpoint/locked-ETH channels, decodes and updates the packed request metadata word, and uses `externalCallBindTo` for each payout. `two_claim_batch_observe` checks two ordered payouts; `every_revert_restores_snapshot` is universal over list lengths and failures.
- **Kill-line** `AddressSourceMutants.admittedFixedOwnerGated`/`runFixedOwnerGated` add a fixed, unrenamed `caller == fixedOwner` gate directly to this file's own `admitted`/`run`/`renameInput`; `fixed_owner_gate_kill_line_refutes_parent` negates the registered parent's exact predicate shape for that mutant (both conclusion conjuncts falsified on one `requestWithdrawals` witness). `fixed_owner_gate_not_admission_equivariant` is the weaker admission-projection sibling, retained as supporting evidence. (The prior `AddressAdmission.ownerGated` toy mutant is retained only as evidence for the separate `P-ADDRESS-1.denote-admission` row — it never touches `SolidityAddress`, so it does not refute this parent.) Source fact `owner_gated_admission_mutant_counterexample` independently shows the same gate shape on pinned `requestWithdrawals` admission.

## Proof

**Abstract `universal_address_writer_equivariance`.** Since wave 5 the parent is unconditional over the four modeled writers: the proof is the direct pair `source_admission_nondiscriminatory` / `source_success_post_state_equivariant`, which hold for every `EntryPoint` via `run_rename` (the `claimWithdrawalsTo` request-owner gate renames with the caller, so the swap is equivariant there too). The wave-4 `hScope : addressEquivarianceEntryScope inp.entryPoint` premise became vacuous when the scope widened to total (`addressEquivarianceEntryScope_total`) — deleting the binder cannot break the proof — so per the wave-4 load-bearing criterion it is no longer carried. `wellFormedAddressInput` and `singletonActorEntryPoint` are likewise not parameters: neither restricts the conclusion (the proof needs only `a₁, a₂ ≠ 0`), and `singletonActorEntryPoint` is provably `False` for every `EntryPoint` in this model (`not_singleton_actor_entry_point`), so requiring its negation added no content while reading as a real exclusion.

**Kill-line.** `fixed_owner_gate_kill_line_refutes_parent` mutates the registered parent's own `admitted` on `requestWithdrawals`/`unwrap` to additionally require `caller = fixedOwner`, a hard-coded address that `renameInput` never touches (unlike the per-request `requestOwner` field, which is renamed and so stays equivariant), and negates the parent's exact predicate shape on that mutant (`runFixedOwnerGated` substituted for `run`, both conclusion conjuncts). Witness: caller `1` (the fixed owner) is admitted on an otherwise-eligible `requestWithdrawals` input and commits `⟨1, 1, 1, 1⟩`; after the `1 ↔ 2` swap, caller `2` is rejected, so the admission bits differ AND the swapped run cannot commit the renamed post `⟨2, 2, 2, 2⟩`. Both failures are `decide`-checked; the refutation closes through the post-state conjunct. The admission-only projection `fixed_owner_gate_not_admission_equivariant` is retained as sibling evidence, subsumed by this kill-line. This directly falsifies the conclusion `universal_address_writer_equivariance` proves, unlike the disconnected `AddressAdmission` toy denotation used previously.

**VERITY `abstract_source_verity_tx_address_equivariance`.** Composes universal source swap, pinned four-entrypoint correspondence, amount/bounds observe = `sourceAddressView`, revert rollback, and renamed-post observe = `postAddressView`. Unaffected by this remediation.

## Issues

The wave-3 branch had already fixed this (`b07e40b`), but that commit never landed on main: PR #131 was closed after only the kill-line half merged.

## Resolution

**Restated Lean/English.** Wave 1 folds well-formedness and singleton-actor exclusion into the registered parent, documents pause/balance/allowance admission on scoped paths, and registers the owner-gate kill line. (`claimWithdrawalsTo` remained in `fidelity.missing` until wave 5 closed it.)

**Wave 3 fix (2026-08-19).** The Wave 1/2 kill-line (`AddressAdmission.ownerGateKillLine_holds`) refuted only a disconnected, audit-authored toy `FunctionSpec` (`AddressAdmission.claim`/`ownerGated`), never `SolidityAddress.run`, so it did not actually refute the registered parent. Replaced the parent-facing kill-line with `AddressSourceMutants.fixed_owner_gate_not_admission_equivariant`, a one-conjunct mutation of this file's own `admitted`/`run`/`renameInput`. The toy fact is retained only as evidence for the separate `P-ADDRESS-1.denote-admission` subordinate row.

**Wave 4 fix (2026-08-19).** Ports the un-landed wave-3 branch commit `b07e40b`. `_hWellFormed` and `_hNotSingleton` are dropped from the parent (neither was load-bearing; the latter's negation is vacuous since `singletonActorEntryPoint` is `False` on every entrypoint here); `hScope` is kept and genuinely threaded through a case split that excludes `claimWithdrawalsTo` via `hScope.elim`, so the parent still makes no claim about that entrypoint and the proof fails to compile if `hScope` is removed. YAML summary/fidelity now describe the scope premise as genuinely used and record in `fidelity.missing` that singleton-actor exclusion is not modeled. (Historical: wave 5 widened the scope to all four modeled writers, making `hScope` vacuous, and retired the premise together with that YAML description — see the wave-5 entry below.)

**Wave 5 fix (2026-08-19).** Two changes. (1) *Parent-shaped kill-line.* The wave-4 kill-line `fixed_owner_gate_not_admission_equivariant` negated only the ADMISSION conjunct of the mutant, without the parent's `hScope` premise and without the post-state conjunct — the shape of the sibling `source_admission_nondiscriminatory`, not of the registered parent. The new `fixed_owner_gate_kill_line_refutes_parent` negates the parent's FULL predicate shape on the mutant `runFixedOwnerGated` (same nonzero-caller binders, BOTH conclusion conjuncts). The witness is an otherwise-eligible `requestWithdrawals` input with callers `1` (the fixed owner) and `2`: the mutant admits caller `1` and commits `⟨1, 1, 1, 1⟩`, while the `1 ↔ 2`-swapped input reverts (`2 ≠ fixedOwner`), so the admission bits differ AND the swapped run cannot commit the renamed post `⟨2, 2, 2, 2⟩`. Both failures are `decide`-checked; the refutation closes through the post-state conjunct. The admission-only theorem is retained as honestly-labeled sibling evidence (it refutes the admission projection and is subsumed by the parent kill-line). (2) *Scope widening.* `addressEquivarianceEntryScope` now covers `claimWithdrawalsTo`: its request-owner gate (`caller = requestOwner`) is caller-relative and renames with the caller (`requestOwner` is an address-indexed input field renamed by `renameInput`), so `run_rename` and its corollaries already hold there, and the Verity side already covers the entrypoint (`claim_observable_correspondence`, `NotRequestOwner`). The scope is therefore total over the four modeled writers (`addressEquivarianceEntryScope_total`). The wave-4 `hScope` premise thereby became vacuous — the source lemmas are unconditional over the four `EntryPoint` constructors, so deleting the binder cannot break the proof — and per the wave-4 load-bearing criterion the registered parent drops it: `universal_address_writer_equivariance` is now unconditional over the four permissionless writers, and the kill-line follows the premise-free shape. No new hypotheses were added; no `sorry`/`admit`/`native_decide`. `fidelity.missing` drops the `claimWithdrawalsTo` exclusion (the singleton-actor modeling gap remains, keeping the row `IMPLEMENTATION_PENDING`); `EXPECTED_CANONICAL_DETAIL_SHA256["P-ADDRESS-1"]` was recomputed and the views regenerated. Note on statement shape: the wave-5 kill-line was first landed against the wave-4 parent WITH the scope implication discharged on the in-scope witness (commit `3d6addc`); the widening then retired the premise, and the kill-line was reshaped to match the premise-free parent, preserving the invariant that the kill-line negates the registered parent's exact predicate shape.

**Wave 6 increment (2026-08-21).** Added `AddressClaimBatchTx` as a separate live-shaped transaction plane. It pins the five unstructured storage constants, reads two-word request and checkpoint records through keyed Verity channels, decodes the exact Solidity request metadata packing, checks the live claim guards, iterates parallel request/hint lists, marks each claimed byte, decrements locked ETH, and executes a journaled value-bearing empty-calldata payout frame. The registered module exposes a two-item observe receipt plus universal failed-run rollback. A fixed-destination payout mutant is rejected by the journal. This does not alter the universal four-projection parent and does not claim physical keccak slot derivation, unbounded source/equivariance correspondence, EnumerableSet/events, or adversarial callee semantics.
